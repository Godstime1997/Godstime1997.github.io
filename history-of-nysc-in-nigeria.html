The History of NYSC in Nigeria: The Khaki That United A Broken Nation

Quick Summary: After the Nigerian Civil War ended in 1970, Nigeria was broken. General Yakubu Gowon needed something to bring youths together. On 22nd May 1973, he created NYSC by Decree No. 24.

After the Nigerian Civil War ended in 1970, Nigeria was broken. There was hatred and distrust. General Yakubu Gowon needed something to bring Nigerian youths together.

On 22nd May 1973, he created NYSC by Decree No. 24 (now NYSC Act). The purpose was to rebuild Nigeria and promote national unity.

Chapter 1: The Beginning (1973)

The first set was 2,000 corpers from 7 universities. They served in all 12 states then. They earned N200 allowance - big money that time!

Gowon's vision: "To properly encourage and develop common ties among the youths of Nigeria and the promotion of national unity".

Chapter 2: How NYSC Has Grown

From 2,000 in 1973 to over 350,000 corpers today every year. From 12 states to 36 states + FCT. 37 orientation camps.

Today NYSC has: DG, 6 Departments, 37 State Coordinators, and Liaison Office in Lagos.

Chapter 3: All NYSC Director Generals (1973-Today)

From Col. Ahmadu Ali (first DG 1973) to Brig. Gen. YD Ahmed (current DG since 2023). Over 22 DGs have led NYSC.

Chapter 4: The 4 Cardinal Programs

1. Orientation (3 weeks): Camp, drills, Man O War, SAED
2. PPA (Primary Assignment): Where you work - school, hospital, ministry
3. Community Development (CDS): Every Wednesday, you help your community
4. Passing Out (POP): Certificate, celebration, end of service

Chapter 5: NYSC Allowance History

1973: N200, 1980s: N250, 1990s: N800, 2000s: N9,800, 2011: N19,800, 2020: N33,000, Today: N77,000

Why NYSC Still Matters

Despite calls to scrap it, NYSC still brings Igbo to serve in Kano, Hausa to serve in Enugu, Yoruba to serve in Bayelsa. It is the only time many Nigerians leave their region for the first time.
